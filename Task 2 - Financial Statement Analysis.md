# Tata Steel — Financial Statement Analysis

## Objective

Analysed Tata Steel's financial performance from FY2023 to FY2025, focusing on revenue, Cost of Goods Sold (COGS), gross profit, operating expenses, EBITDA and EBIT to identify key performance trends and business drivers.

## Period Covered

FY2023, FY2024 and FY2025

## Analysis Performed

- Analysed year-on-year movement in revenue from operations.
- Analysed major components of COGS.
- Evaluated gross profit and gross margin trends.
- Analysed Selling, General & Administrative (SG&A) expenses.
- Evaluated EBITDA and EBITDA growth.
- Analysed depreciation and amortisation.
- Evaluated EBIT and EBIT growth.
- Identified operational and market factors driving major changes.

## Revenue Analysis

Revenue from operations declined from ₹1,42,913.32 crore in FY2023 to ₹1,32,516.66 crore in FY2025.

In FY2025, revenue from sale of products declined by approximately 6% compared with the previous year. The analysis attributes this primarily to lower selling-price realisations in domestic and export markets, influenced by cheaper Chinese steel imports.

The impact was partially offset by higher steel deliveries, which increased by approximately 1.03 million tonnes.

## COGS Analysis

COGS declined from ₹75,674.63 crore in FY2023 to ₹63,766.43 crore in FY2025.

The major components analysed included:

- Cost of materials consumed
- Power, fuel and industrial gases
- Conversion charges
- Purchases of stock-in-trade
- Changes in inventories

The reduction in COGS contributed to the improvement in gross margin.

## Gross Profit & Margin

Gross profit increased from ₹67,238.69 crore in FY2023 to ₹72,977.08 crore in FY2024 before declining to ₹68,750.23 crore in FY2025.

Despite the decline in absolute gross profit in FY2025, gross margin remained broadly stable at approximately 51.88%, compared with 51.78% in FY2024.

## Operating Expense Analysis

The analysis covered major operating expenses including:

- Freight and handling charges
- Commissions, discounts and rebates
- Selling and marketing expenses
- Stores and spares
- Repairs
- Royalty
- General expenses
- Salaries and wages
- Staff welfare expenses
- Administrative expenses

Selling and marketing expenses increased by approximately 14.47% in FY2025, while total SG&A expenses declined by approximately 2.01%.

## Key Expense Drivers

### Salaries and Wages

Salaries and wages increased by approximately 5.3% in FY2025. The analysis links this increase to salary revisions and their consequential impact on employee retirement provisions.

### Staff Welfare Expenses

Staff welfare expenses increased significantly, partly reflecting the commissioning and operationalisation of Tata Steel's Kalinganagar Phase II expansion and associated welfare infrastructure.

### Freight and Handling

Freight and handling costs increased as steel deliveries increased by approximately 1.03 million tonnes, resulting in higher logistics requirements.

### Commissions, Discounts and Rebates

Commissions, discounts and rebates increased as Tata Steel pushed higher sales volumes in a softer pricing environment.

### Rent and Hire Charges

Rent and hire charges increased by approximately 22%, reflecting logistics and supply-chain requirements as well as infrastructure associated with capacity expansion.

## EBITDA Analysis

EBITDA increased from ₹31,644.82 crore in FY2023 to ₹36,858.91 crore in FY2024.

In FY2025, EBITDA declined to ₹33,165.76 crore, representing approximately a 10.02% decline from FY2024.

This reflects the impact of lower revenue realisations and operating pressures despite continued cost management.

## Depreciation & Amortisation

Depreciation and amortisation increased from ₹6,008.95 crore in FY2024 to ₹6,253.16 crore in FY2025.

The analysis links the increase to the capitalisation of major facilities associated with the Tata Steel Kalinganagar Phase II expansion.

## EBIT Analysis

EBIT increased from ₹25,688.50 crore in FY2023 to ₹30,849.96 crore in FY2024.

In FY2025, EBIT declined to ₹26,912.60 crore, representing approximately a 12.76% decline from FY2024.

## Strategic / Operational Driver

A recurring driver identified in the analysis is the commissioning of Tata Steel's Kalinganagar Phase II expansion.

Major facilities were commissioned during FY2025, including:

- India's largest blast furnace
- Continuous Annealing Line
- Coke Plant #2

As these assets were commissioned, related expenditure transitioned from capitalisation to normal operating expenses and depreciation.

## Key Takeaways

- Revenue declined in FY2025, primarily due to lower realisations.
- COGS declined significantly over the three-year period.
- Gross margin remained broadly stable despite lower revenue.
- EBITDA and EBIT declined in FY2025 after strong growth in FY2024.
- Employee, logistics and expansion-related costs were important expense drivers.
- Kalinganagar Phase II commissioning influenced depreciation and operating-cost patterns.
